## 07/11 (Tipo A, 11:00) · The Biggest Ship on the Great Lakes Sent Its Last Message at 7:10 PM · ficha 02
- **Duração estimada:** 47 s, 160 palavras

**Ganchos**
- A) The captain said they were fine. Minutes later, the ship was gone. (ficha; "captain" não é certo: descartado)
- B) Her last message said: We are holding our own. Minutes later, she was gone. **(escolhido: verdadeiro e direto)**
- C) Twenty-nine men vanished on Lake Superior, and nobody called for help.

**Narração**

Her last message said: We are holding our own. Minutes later, she was gone.

The Edmund Fitzgerald was seven hundred and twenty-nine feet long, the biggest ship on the Great Lakes when she was launched. On November tenth, 1975, she was crossing Lake Superior with twenty-nine men, in a storm that kept getting worse.

At three thirty, she reported a list, and damage on deck. Then she lost both radars. So another ship, the Arthur M. Anderson, became her eyes, ten miles behind her, reading her position out over the radio. At ten past seven, they asked how she was doing. She said she was holding her own.

Then the snow lifted. The men on the Anderson could see lights twenty miles away. The Fitzgerald should have been right there, ten miles ahead.

There was nothing. Nothing on the radar. No mayday. No wreckage in sight.

The Coast Guard blamed the hatches. The full video shows why many still disagree.

## 06/11 (Tipo B, 10:00) · Who Was the Man Who Never Was, and Why Did Hitler Believe Him? · curiosidade 3
- **Duração estimada:** 49 s, 167 palavras

**Ganchos**
- A) A homeless man died in London. Weeks later, Hitler's army trusted him as a Royal Marines officer. **(escolhido: da ficha, paradoxo imediato)**
- B) The most important officer in this operation had never existed.
- C) Britain fooled Hitler with a briefcase and a love letter.

**Narração**

A homeless man died in London. Months later, Hitler's army trusted him as a Royal Marines officer.

His name was Glyndwr Michael, a Welshman. In January 1943, British intelligence had a problem: how do you tell Germany the wrong place to expect an invasion? So they gave him a new name. Major William Martin, who did not exist.

They built him a life. A photo of a fiancee. Bills. And a briefcase chained to him, full of false plans.

On April thirtieth, a British submarine let him go in the water near Huelva, in Spain. Spanish officials passed copies of the papers to German intelligence. By May fourteenth, British codebreakers could see that the Germans believed it.

Hitler moved forces to Greece and Sardinia, including a whole Panzer division. On July ninth, the Allies landed in Sicily. Historians still argue how much of that move was this trick.

He was buried as a major. It took until the 1990s for his real name to reach the stone.

**Notas:** Corrigi "Weeks later" da ficha para "Months later" (janeiro a maio de 1943). "Homeless" e a identidade seguem a ficha; não cito causa da morte. O título diz "Hitler" e o texto ressalva o mérito (MÉDIA). Lápide: 1997 ou 1998, por isso "the 1990s".

## 08/11 (Tipo B, 10:00) · Why Did Germany Give This Spy the Iron Cross, When He Was Working for Britain? · curiosidade 4
- **Duração estimada:** 50 s, 169 palavras

**Ganchos**
- A) Hitler's spy network in Britain had 27 agents. Not one of them existed. **(escolhido: número e virada em uma linha)**
- B) Germany and Britain both gave this man a medal for the same lie.
- C) He applied to be a British spy. They said no. So he started anyway.

**Narração**

Germany's spy network in Britain had twenty-seven agents. Not one of them existed.

They all came from one Spaniard, Juan Pujol Garcia. He offered to spy for the British, and they turned him down. So he made himself into a pro-Nazi agent instead, and sent Germany reports from Lisbon, written out of guidebooks and newsreels.

It worked so well that the Germans sent forces after a convoy that was not there. Only then, in April 1942, did MI5 hire him.

His imaginary network kept growing. In 1944, he told Berlin the Normandy landings were only a diversion, and that the real blow was still coming. He claimed about seventy-five Allied divisions, when there were around fifty.

That year, Germany gave him the Iron Cross. Britain made him a member of the Order of the British Empire. Same man, same lie.

In 1949, he faked his own death and moved to Venezuela. By one count, Berlin had paid him about three hundred and forty thousand dollars to be lied to.

**Notas:** "Hitler's" virou "Germany's" na frase: a rede era do Abwehr. US$ 340 mil e 75 divisões são MÉDIA, por isso "by one count" e "claimed". Não uso "um dos dois honrados pelos dois lados" (BAIXA).

## 09/11 (Tipo B, 10:00) · How Did a Medic Who Refused to Carry a Rifle Earn the Medal of Honor? · curiosidade 8
- **Duração estimada:** 43 s, 144 palavras

**Ganchos**
- A) He refused to touch a rifle. The Army gave him its highest medal. **(escolhido: paradoxo em duas frases)**
- B) Nobody on this cliff had less reason to stay.
- C) He would not call himself a conscientious objector.

**Narração**

He refused to touch a rifle. The Army gave him its highest medal.

Desmond Doss was a Seventh-day Adventist who joined the Army as a combat medic, and went to war without a weapon. He did not like the term conscientious objector. He called himself a conscientious cooperator.

In 1945, on Okinawa, his unit was fighting on the Maeda Escarpment, a cliff that Americans called Hacksaw Ridge. Doss is credited with saving between seventy-five and one hundred wounded men there. Nobody knows the exact number. His citation just says many.

He was wounded four times on Okinawa. Later, tuberculosis took one of his lungs and five of his ribs.

On October twelfth, 1945, President Truman presented him the Medal of Honor. Doss was the first of three conscientious objectors to receive it.

He carried no weapon. He carried about seventy-five men, and possibly more.
